RC Car

Zero-cost rebuild with salvaged hardware and 3D-printed chassis

Images/Rc Car/ZoomedIn.jpeg

Description

Rebuilt an RC vehicle from scratch at zero cost by salvaging existing electronic and mechanical components and designing a custom 3D-printed chassis in SolidWorks. Working around fixed hardware, including the DC motor, LiPo battery, receiver, stock wheels, shocks, and CV axles, I modeled the frame to accommodate the existing components while maintaining structural integrity and simplifying assembly. I designed the chassis for 3D printing, considering print orientation, component fitment, and ease of assembly.

Applied vehicle kinematics principles to the chassis and steering design, incorporating Ackermann steering geometry to reduce tire scrub during turns and refining CV axle alignment to minimize binding and maintain effective power transfer. Through CAD modeling, mechanical integration, and hands-on troubleshooting, I developed a functional RC vehicle using entirely salvaged hardware and free 3D-printing materials. Additional documentation, including project development and build details, is available on my Instagram.

Constraints

  • Zero budget — no money spent on any parts or materials
  • Limited to existing salvaged hardware: DC motor, LiPo battery, receiver/transceiver, stock wheels, shocks, and CV axles
  • Only manufacturing tool available was a 3D printer

Goals

  • Rebuild a fully functional RC vehicle using only salvaged components and free 3D-printing materials
  • Design a custom 3D-printed chassis in SolidWorks optimized for print orientation and assembly
  • Incorporate Ackermann steering geometry to reduce tire scrub during turns
  • Refine CV axle alignment to minimize binding and maintain effective power transfer
  • Accommodate all existing hardware within the new frame
