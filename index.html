Formula SAE Suspension — Clean-Sheet Design

Suspension Team Lead · Rice Formula SAE · 2025–Present

Rice's first Formula SAE car has no prior-year vehicle to iterate from, so the suspension was designed from nothing: no inherited geometry, no internal data, no known-good baseline to fall back on. I set the handling targets first and placed every hardpoint to hit them, rather than fitting the suspension around whatever the chassis left over.

Cost drove the manufacturing decisions as hard as the loads drove the geometry. Tube sizes, weld joints, and tolerances were all specified so the parts could be cut, machined, and welded in-house at the OEDK instead of being sent out.

Quadcopter Built to a $400 Ceiling

Independent build · Design, fabrication, electronics

A drone designed around what I could afford rather than what would be ideal. Carbon fiber was out, so the entire frame is printed in PA nylon and the geometry had to do the work the material wouldn't — arm cross-sections and joint design iterated across print revisions to hold rigidity at the motor mounts without adding mass.

The electrical side taught me the most. Sizing motors, ESCs, propellers, and battery against all-up weight, then wiring and debugging power distribution and signal routing end to end, turned a parts list into a system I understood.

Wearable IMU Study of Post-Stroke Movement

Undergraduate Research Assistant · CHRP Lab, Rice University · Summer 2026

The lab is working on a question that sounds simple and isn't: when someone recovering from a stroke reaches for a cup, how does that movement differ from an able-bodied reach? Clinical assessments catch the obvious differences in a lab setting. What's missing is data on unscripted, everyday movement — the compensatory strategies people actually develop at home.

My work was the data pipeline. I instrumented participants, ran recording sessions, and wrote the processing that made the raw output usable: filtering sensor noise, correcting drift, and producing clean signals that could be segmented into individual movements. I also built an iOS app so sessions could run without an engineer standing over them.

RC Car for Effectively Nothing

Independent build · First full CAD-to-fabrication project

The constraint here was total: salvaged electronics out of a donor vehicle and donated filament for every printed part. The motor, ESC, and battery form factors were fixed and unmodifiable, so the chassis and drivetrain had to be packaged around hardware I had no ability to change.

This was the first project I took all the way from a SolidWorks model to a printed, assembled, driving vehicle. Everything I've built since is a version of that same loop with higher stakes.
